This reference artifact brings together all seven proto-personas representing our customers, supply-side drivers, and internal operations.

---

## 1. Customer Personas

### Stranded Sarah (The Stranded Survivor)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** She is stressed, stranded on a highway, and potentially in danger. She is opening the app to request an emergency tow.


* 
**Physical Environment:** She is standing on the shoulder of a rainy highway, experiencing a loud and chaotic environment. She is staring at her screen in harsh lighting or bad weather. Later, she is physically and emotionally exhausted while riding in the cab of the tow truck or trailing behind in a ride-share.


* 
**Primary Goal:** She requires speed, low cognitive load, and high transparency, such as live tracking. She ultimately wants a frictionless exit with a clear, itemized digital receipt without hidden fees.


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Lengthy forms or complex questions cause high frustration.


* A delayed confirmation SMS will drastically spike her anxiety.


* Low-contrast UI makes updates impossible to read through rain or sun glare.


* A lagging GPS map or fluctuating ETA shatters trust.


* A mobile waiver with tiny text prevents her from signing quickly to get to safety.


* A buggy live route tracker that fails to stream accurately reignites her panic.


* A clunky Bluetooth reader or complicated payment gateway prevents a frictionless exit.


* Discovering hidden fees instead of a clear itemized breakdown causes sticker shock.





### Careful Carl (The Careful Planner)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** He needs a classic car moved or a broken-down vehicle transported from a driveway to a mechanic.


* 
**Physical Environment:** He initiates the flow while sitting comfortably at a desk or on his couch, taking his time to evaluate his options. He goes about his normal day in a relaxed state. Later, he is safely standing in his own driveway or a residential street overseeing the hook-up. Finally, he might drive his primary vehicle behind the tow truck to meet the driver at the destination in a relaxed environment.


* 
**Primary Goal:** He experiences lower stress and prioritizes careful handling alongside accurate scheduling. Success for him relies on a UI that focuses on upfront pricing, trust signals, and precise calendar scheduling.


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Unclear equipment details will cause him to abandon the form.


* Unclear upfront pricing and scheduling will make him look elsewhere.


* Lack of precise push notifications makes it difficult to time his walk to the driveway.


* A driver rushing the hook-up process triggers anxiety about vehicle safety.


* Feeling pressured to sign before reading the waiver thoroughly causes friction in documentation.


* Passive GPS links failing to update seamlessly makes coordinating arrival times frustrating.


* The final charge not matching the upfront pricing agreed upon creates a "bait and switch" feeling.


* A poor or blurry final drop-off photo failing to adequately prove the car was delivered without damage.





### Reluctant Ricky (The Reluctant Retriever)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** He needs to recover his vehicle after an involuntary tow. He requires clarity, is likely stressed, and is potentially highly agitated or frustrated.


* 
**Physical Environment:** Initially, he is standing in an empty parking space where he previously left his car. He must then physically travel to an impound lot to deal with a clerk behind a glass window, bypassing the side-of-the-road mobile experience entirely.


* 
**Primary Goal:** He needs simplicity in terms of verifying possession of his car, the pick-up location, and pricing. He needs an app design that aggressively prioritizes the impound lot's exact location, provides clear instructions on required documentation like ID and registration, and features a frictionless payment gateway.


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Struggling to frantically search the app to verify possession and locate the exact impound lot.


* Encountering a UI that is not completely devoid of marketing fluff.

## 2. Supply-Side (Tower) Personas

### Fleet-Driver Frank (The Fleet Driver)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** He is an employee of a large towing company receiving a job alert while potentially driving or finishing up a previous call. He wants to get through his queue efficiently and is highly motivated by closing tickets quickly to move to the next job.


* 
**Physical Environment:** He is operating heavy machinery, dealing with traffic, and working in potentially poor weather. He wears heavy work gloves, has his phone mounted on a vibrating dashboard, and moves between a bright exterior and a dark truck cab. He might be using mounted, older, or ruggedized company devices sitting in a sun-glared cab.


* 
**Primary Goal:** He hates administrative busywork and requires an interface with heavy automation, utilizing features like auto-filled fields and one-tap status updates. He wants a frictionless payment handoff so he can leave immediately to keep the dispatch system from failing.


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Tiny "Accept" buttons while driving cause intense UI friction.


* A lack of high-contrast dark mode options creates visual fatigue and glare at night.


* Slow photo upload tools or tools requiring too many taps while in traffic create clunky, frustrating workflows.


* A lack of voice-to-text or one-tap options for notes slows down his hook-up phase.


* An app requiring manual input instead of seamlessly handling GPS streaming in the background disrupts his transport routing.


* Friction in the payment handoff causes departure delays and prevents him from leaving immediately.





### Owner-Operator Oscar (The Owner-Operator)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** He is an independent tower opening the app because accepting a job is a direct business decision for him.


* 
**Physical Environment:** He drives carefully with his own equipment while dealing with a vibrating dashboard and harsh lighting. During the arrival and hook-up phases, he faces the same harsh physical environment involving gloves, weather, and traffic.


* 
**Primary Goal:** He cares deeply about the payment phase, digital invoicing, and customer ratings because these elements directly impact his bottom line. He needs robust financial summaries and easy ways to dispute reviews or prompt happy customers for ratings. He must quickly capture legally compliant pre-tow photos and digital waivers because his personal liability is highly elevated.


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Job alerts failing to instantly highlight payout, distance, and job type prevent him from making profitable decisions.


* Unreliable live tracking fails to reassure the customer, which directly threatens his eventual rating.


* A camera interface that is not rock-solid, fast, and legally compliant slows down his hook-up and exposes him to personal liability.


* A hands-on UI distracting him from safely transporting the vehicle with his own equipment.


* A flawless Bluetooth card reader failing creates severe financial bottlenecks.


* The system failing to email the automated PDF receipt and feedback link ruins his financial success and reputation.

## 3. Operational & B2B Personas

### Orchestrator Olivia (The Orchestrator / Dispatcher)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** She is translating the customer's panic or the web form's data into actionable logistics from a desktop interface. The happy path workflow heavily relies on her Central Desktop Dashboard to keep the customer and driver synchronized.


* 
**Physical Environment:** She relies heavily on her Central Desktop Dashboard. Her attention rapidly moves on to the next caller, meaning her environment demands automated GPS updates without manual intervention.


* 
**Primary Goal:** Her goals include maximizing fleet efficiency, minimizing driver downtime, and resolving customer escalations quickly. Her mindset is highly analytical, perpetually multitasking, and heavily reliant on keyboard shortcuts. She needs the drop-off and payment phase to be completely automated so the dashboard seamlessly closes out the invoice and resets the driver to "Available".


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Workflow bottlenecks caused by a desktop interface failing to quickly identify drivetrains or closest flatbeds instantly.


* A lack of keyboard shortcuts to fire off API push notifications.


* Having to manually update the customer's live map if the continuous GPS stream fails to automate it.


* Data blindspots caused by drivers forgetting to update statuses, and dashboards lacking clear visual indicators of driver bottlenecks.


* System failures resulting in the software failing to handle GPS routing entirely in the background.


* Administrative friction preventing the system from automatically closing the invoice and resetting the driver status, which destroys fleet efficiency.





### B2B Brian (The B2B Initiator)

* 
**Scenario/Trigger:** He initiates the "Car Was Towed" scenario. He bypasses the consumer intake web form entirely and uses an authoritative "flagging" system.


* 
**Physical Environment:** He is standing in a parking lot, an apartment complex, or on a public street. Once the dispatcher receives his B2B request and dispatches a driver, his journey essentially ends.


* 
**Primary Goal:** His mindset is focused on compliance, liability protection, and speed. His specific goals are to quickly log an illegally parked car, upload photo evidence to justify the tow, and get the vehicle removed without dealing with the vehicle's owner directly. His success is defined solely by the vehicle being logged securely and removed quickly to protect his liability.


* 
**Key Frustrations:** * Any intake friction that slows down his ability to quickly log an illegally parked car.


* App issues that slow down his ability to efficiently upload photo evidence.


* System friction that delays getting the vehicle removed from the property quickly.
